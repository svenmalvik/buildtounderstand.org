What if some forms of life aren't rare, but just invisible to us?

A zeptosecond is to one second what one second is to 31.7 trillion years which is about 2000 times the age of the universe. Scientists have actually measured an event lasting 247 zeptoseconds: the time light took to cross a hydrogen molecule.

At that scale, a complete human-like lifetime could happen in a few picoseconds. Entire generations might appear and disappear without us ever noticing because we recognize their effects as noise simply because we don't know where to look, what to measure, or even what to ask.

In platform engineering, we don't always see what's right in front of us either. We may not recognize a platform problem until the same signal occur twice or more. Teams ask for access. Finance wants to know which team created the cost. Compliance needs data controls. Engineers need evaluations and observability.

When that repeated work becomes visible, the answer can seem simple:

> Add an Ai gateway. Create a specifc Ai portal. Or centralize more. <

Each addition may solve a specific problem. However, each new solution also creates new work which someone must take care of over time.

That made me wonder whether the right AI platform could be almost invisible; ideally on zeptosecond-level 😄
