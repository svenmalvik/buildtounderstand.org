# What Should an AI Agent Leave Behind After Completing a Task?

## Question

An agent contract describes what should be known before an agent runs: its purpose, owner, risk, permissions, evaluation, and operational references. But what should remain after it runs?

Is a successful result enough? What must another person be able to understand or verify? Should an agent preserve its actions, evidence, costs, tool calls, and uncertainty? How much evidence is useful before it becomes expensive surveillance and storage?

The answer may also depend on what the agent was allowed to do. A read-only agent that summarizes internal information doesn't create the same risk as an agent that moves money or changes production infrastructure. The greater the authority and possible impact, and the harder the work is to reverse, the more important its evidence may become.

## Exploration

I want to understand what makes autonomous work trustworthy after it is done.

More logs don't necessarily create more trust. A complete activity log may be too detailed to review, expensive to retain, and still fail to explain why the result should be trusted. A short explanation may be easier to read but omit the evidence needed to verify it.

The useful artifact may need to connect the result to the agent that produced it, the authority and information it used, the changes it made, and the evidence used to verify the outcome. It may also need to show whether the work can be reversed or reproduced.

This raises several questions:

- What did the agent do?
- What authority and information did it use?
- Why should someone trust the result?
- What changed?
- Can the work be reviewed, reversed, or reproduced?
- What must remain portable so a team can change platforms without losing its operational history?
- Who benefits from retaining this evidence, who maintains it, and when should it be removed?

The goal is not to record everything. I want to find the smallest useful record that makes one completed task understandable without creating more work than it removes.

## Prototype

I will test a small, portable receipt generated by an agent after completing one task. The first version could look like this:

```yaml
task:
  agent: settlement-explainer
  revision: a8c21f4
  requested_by: sven
  purpose: explain settlement deviation

execution:
  model: approved-eu-provider
  tools_used:
    - settlement-reader
  trace: trace-123
  cost: 0.18

result:
  outcome: explanation-produced
  evidence:
    - settlement-8472
  changes: []
  verification: passed
  reversible: true
```

The experiment isn't about designing a complete schema. It is about testing whether this receipt helps another person understand and verify the work. It should also reveal which information belongs in the receipt, which information should remain in the systems that produced it, and whether the receipt removes repeated review, incident, and audit work.
