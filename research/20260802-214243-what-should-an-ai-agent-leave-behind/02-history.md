# History and precedent: what autonomous work should leave behind

## Long arc

The proposed “agent receipt” belongs to a much older family of attempts to make delegated work answerable after the worker, process, or machine has moved on. Traditional audit trails preserve a sequence of security-relevant events; their value is forensic and institutional, not merely operational. NIST’s log-management guide, published on 2006-09-13, treats generation, transmission, storage, access, analysis, and disposal as one management problem—not “write some text to a file”—and recommends integrity protection because a log controlled by the system being investigated can be altered with that system. Early cryptographic audit-log work by Bruce Schneier and John Kelsey made the adversarial point explicit: logs on an untrusted machine need cryptographic protection so pre-compromise entries cannot be changed or destroyed without detection. The durable lesson is that a record is not evidence merely because it exists; provenance, custody, integrity, and interpretation determine what it can prove. [NIST SP 800-92](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/92/final) [Schneier and Kelsey, “Secure Audit Logs to Support Computer Forensics”](https://www.schneier.com/academic/archives/1999/05/secure_audit_logs_to.html)

Software engineering then separated two questions that raw audit trails tend to mix. Event sourcing records **how state changed** by making every state transition an event from which prior state can be reconstructed; Martin Fowler’s 2005-12-12 description emphasizes rebuild, temporal query, and replay. Architecture Decision Records record **why a consequential choice was made**; Michael Nygard’s 2011-11-15 template preserves context, decision, status, and consequences in a short file beside the system. An agent receipt is trying to cover parts of both: it wants an operational account of what changed and a compact explanation of purpose and verification. History warns that one compact document rarely serves both perfectly. [Fowler, “Event Sourcing”](https://www.martinfowler.com/eaaDev/EventSourcing.html) [Nygard, “Documenting Architecture Decisions”](https://www.cognitect.com/blog/2011/11/15/documenting-architecture-decisions)

Distributed systems added a third form: the trace. Google’s Dapper linked work across services into causal span trees and deliberately used sampling and common-library instrumentation to keep overhead low. The paper says Dapper’s measure of success was usefulness to developer and operations teams and describes how a tracing tool became a platform for unanticipated analysis tools. OpenTelemetry later turned that pattern into vendor-neutral APIs, protocols, collectors, and semantic conventions; W3C Trace Context standardized propagation across service boundaries. This lineage offers portability and causal reconstruction, but it optimizes for observability. A trace can show model calls, tool invocations, latency, tokens, and errors without proving that the record is complete, untampered, policy-compliant, independently verified, or understandable to a non-operator. [Google, “Dapper”](https://research.google/pubs/dapper-a-large-scale-distributed-systems-tracing-infrastructure/) [OpenTelemetry overview](https://opentelemetry.io/docs/what-is-opentelemetry/) [W3C Trace Context](https://www.w3.org/TR/trace-context/)

Provenance work addressed the interchange problem. The W3C PROV recommendations published on 2013-04-30 model entities, activities, agents, derivation, use, generation, and attribution so provenance can move between heterogeneous systems. In parallel, AI documentation artifacts such as Datasheets for Datasets and Model Cards made compact, structured disclosure a design norm: the former records how a dataset was created and should be used; the latter records intended uses, evaluation conditions, limitations, and performance. These are strong precedents for a readable, portable artifact, but they are principally lifecycle or release documents. They do not describe the evidence left by one concrete runtime task. [W3C PROV overview](https://www.w3.org/TR/2013/NOTE-prov-overview-20130430/) [Gebru et al., “Datasheets for Datasets”](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09010) [Mitchell et al., “Model Cards for Model Reporting”](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.03993)

Software-supply-chain security added the missing integrity layer. in-toto records what steps were performed, by whom, in what order, and against which materials, then signs the layout and link metadata so a consumer can verify the chain. SLSA generalized build provenance and deliberately simplified its stable format after early-adopter feedback, an instructive example of reducing ambition to improve adoption. Sigstore’s Rekor and IETF SCITT go further toward “cryptographic receipts”: an append-only transparency service can return a signed inclusion proof, allowing a relying party to verify that a signed statement was registered. But SCITT is careful about the boundary: registration proves a statement was recorded and passed registration policy; it does not prove the statement’s contents are true. [in-toto specification](https://github.com/in-toto/docs/blob/master/in-toto-spec.md) [in-toto USENIX paper](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity19/presentation/torres-arias) [SLSA stable-release rationale](https://slsa.dev/spec/v1.0/whats-new) [Sigstore Rekor](https://docs.sigstore.dev/logging/overview/) [IETF SCITT architecture](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-ietf-scitt-architecture-22.html)

AI agents now bring these streams together. The EU AI Act’s 2024-07-12 text requires high-risk systems to support automatic event logging appropriate to traceability, and Articles 19 and 26 require relevant providers and deployers to retain automatically generated logs under their control for at least six months, subject to other law. OpenAI’s Agents SDK traces agent runs, turns, generations, tools, guardrails, and handoffs; OpenTelemetry began publishing agent-specific observability guidance on 2025-03-06; A2A models task history and produced artifacts. NIST launched an AI Agent Standards Initiative on 2026-02-17 around interoperability, security, identity, and trusted adoption. Most directly, an individual IETF Internet-Draft published on 2026-04-13 proposes an `AgentInteractionRecord`, independent evidence custodian, hash-chain integrity, sequence completeness, privacy-preserving redaction receipts, and SCITT evidence receipts for material agent actions. That draft is unusually close to the exploration’s premise, but it is an early proposal by P. Emirdag of VERIDIC Inc., not an adopted standard or evidence that the design works in practice. [EU AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=celex%3A32024R1689) [OpenAI Agents SDK tracing](https://openai.github.io/openai-agents-js/guides/tracing/) [OpenTelemetry agent observability](https://opentelemetry.io/blog/2025/ai-agent-observability/) [A2A specification](https://google-a2a.github.io/A2A/specification/) [NIST AI Agent Standards Initiative](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/02/announcing-ai-agent-standards-initiative-interoperable-and-secure) [IETF Internet-Draft: AI Agent Execution Profile of SCITT](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-emirdag-scitt-ai-agent-execution-00)

### Precedent map

| Need in the proposed receipt | Strongest historical precedent | What still has to be invented or chosen |
|---|---|---|
| What changed? | Event sourcing; in-toto link metadata | A domain-neutral way to point to authoritative before/after state without copying it. |
| Why was this task performed? | ADRs; task requests | A distinction between requester intent, agent rationale, and policy authorization. |
| Who or what acted? | W3C PROV; in-toto; signed statements | Stable agent, operator, principal, model, and delegation identities. |
| How did the work unfold? | Dapper/OpenTelemetry traces | Sampling and privacy rules, plus a task-level summary that remains useful without the full trace. |
| Can the record be trusted? | Secure logs; Rekor; SCITT | Independent capture or custody, key management, and proof that omitted actions would be detectable. |
| Was the result correct? | Tests and in-toto policies | Domain-specific verification, verifier identity, criteria, evidence, and uncertainty. |
| Can it travel between platforms? | W3C PROV; OpenTelemetry; in-toto attestations | A small common vocabulary with extensible domain fields and durable evidence references. |

## Failed attempts at the same thing

### 1. Complete-looking logs that omit the event investigators need

Okta’s own incident account says that for 14 days its investigation did not identify suspicious support-system downloads because investigators followed the event type generated when a user opened a case attachment, while the attacker navigated through a Files tab that produced a different record ID. Okta later found a gap missing the attacker’s final hours and additional downloaded files after delayed logs arrived. The failure was not “no logs”; it was an incomplete mental and semantic model of which events represented the action. A receipt field such as `tools_used` has the same weakness if the capture boundary does not see alternate paths. [Okta root-cause report](https://sec.okta.com/articles/2023/11/unauthorized-access-oktas-support-case-management-system-root-cause/)

### 2. Rich logs that are gated, voluminous, and useful only after expert curation

The Cyber Safety Review Board found that the U.S. State Department detected the Microsoft Exchange Online intrusion because it had enhanced logging and had spent three years building custom alert logic. The Board also heard that few organizations analyzed the voluminous `MailItemsAccessed` log in detail and that such analysis would be difficult for smaller organizations. CISA subsequently praised Microsoft for moving more than 30 previously premium log types into the standard tier. This is the clearest precedent for the exploration’s claim that “more logs” need not produce trust: availability, affordability, semantic curation, and reviewer capacity are separate constraints. [CSRB report page](https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/resources/CSRB-Review-Summer-2023-MEO-Intrusion) [Congress-hosted CSRB report](https://www.congress.gov/118/chrg/CHRG-118hhrg58996/CHRG-118hhrg58996.pdf) [CISA on free logging](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/news/when-tech-vendors-make-important-logging-info-available-free-everyone-wins) [Microsoft logging expansion](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2023/07/19/expanding-cloud-logging-to-give-customers-deeper-security-visibility/)

### 3. Audit evidence that exists but is commercially and operationally inaccessible

The Post Office Horizon record shows that the presence of detailed audit data did not make it practically available to the people whose conduct the system purported to describe. The inquiry’s issues include Fujitsu’s ability to alter branch data remotely without subpostmasters’ knowledge and the inability of subpostmasters to identify causes of discrepancies. Evidence before the inquiry records investigators rationing Audit Record Query requests, delaying requests after monthly allowances ran out, and avoiding requests to preserve quota; a Criminal Cases Review Commission statement cites the judgment’s criticism that per-request charges were not an adequate reason to proceed without full audit data. Evidence without affordable, timely, contestable access did not create accountability. [Inquiry provisional issues](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/post-office-horizon-it-inquiry-2020/provisional-list-of-issues) [Inquiry transcript on Audit Record Query constraints](https://postofficeinquiry.dracos.co.uk/phase-4/2023-11-17/) [Criminal Cases Review Commission statement](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6013eb9bd3bf7f70bb9b0096/Statement_005_-_The_Criminal_Cases_Review_Commission.pdf)

### 4. Tamper protection that arrives after the attacker

Recent audit-system research found that inefficient pipelines force many systems to log only a restricted set of events and to protect generated records asynchronously. In a study of 164 proof-of-concept kernel exploits, many left no trace under common audit policies; the researchers also measured a case where a state-of-the-art system required up to 15 milliseconds to protect records while a simple attack altered logs in about 5 milliseconds. The result is a hard systems constraint for agent receipts: post-execution generation cannot prove completeness if the execution or capture pipeline was already compromised. [Gandhi et al., USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/gandhi)

### 5. Cryptographic integrity mistaken for truth

Signed timestamps, append-only ledgers, Rekor entries, and SCITT receipts can show that a particular statement existed and has not been silently rewritten. SCITT explicitly says transparency does not prevent a dishonest or compromised issuer; it makes statements inspectable and issuers accountable. A signed receipt generated from false or selectively captured inputs is still a faithfully preserved false or incomplete statement. The agent receipt therefore needs distinct claims for **record integrity**, **capture completeness**, and **outcome correctness**. [IETF RFC 3161](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/rfc3161/) [Sigstore security model](https://docs.sigstore.dev/about/security/) [IETF SCITT architecture](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-ietf-scitt-architecture-22.html)

## Recurring cast

- **The principal or requester** delegates authority and needs to know whether intent was followed. The new difficulty is preserving the exact delegation boundary rather than only a username.
- **The actor or issuer** produces work and an account of it. In agents this may be a model, runtime, framework, operator, and chain of subagents—not one stable identity. W3C PROV’s separation of agent, activity, and entity is useful here. [W3C PROV-O](https://www.w3.org/TR/2013/PR-prov-o-20130312/)
- **The capture pipeline** observes events. Dapper and OpenTelemetry show why common instrumentation and context propagation matter; Okta shows that an unseen path can evade the expected event vocabulary. [Google Dapper](https://research.google/pubs/dapper-a-large-scale-distributed-systems-tracing-infrastructure/) [OpenTelemetry logging](https://opentelemetry.io/docs/specs/otel/logs/)
- **The custodian or transparency service** preserves records and, in cryptographic systems, proves inclusion and consistency. Independence matters because an operator-controlled log can otherwise be rewritten or selectively disclosed. [IETF SCITT](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-ietf-scitt-architecture-22.html)
- **The verifier** evaluates something more specific than “passed”: policy compliance, result quality, transaction effects, safety, or evidence integrity. in-toto makes the expected layout and authorized functionaries explicit rather than accepting any signed event. [in-toto specification](https://github.com/in-toto/docs/blob/master/in-toto-spec.md)
- **The reviewer, auditor, incident responder, regulator, or affected person** arrives later and may lack platform context or privileged access. Horizon demonstrates why the evidence subject’s ability to obtain and contest the record matters. [Post Office Horizon Inquiry](https://www.gov.uk/government/groups/the-post-office-horizon-it-inquiry)
- **The schema and platform vendors** benefit when their event vocabulary becomes the de facto standard. OpenTelemetry’s vendor-neutral approach and explicit goal that users own generated data are a precedent for preserving engineering freedom. [OpenTelemetry overview](https://opentelemetry.io/docs/what-is-opentelemetry/)
- **Standards and regulatory institutions** increasingly define minimum traceability without prescribing one universal receipt: W3C, IETF, CNCF/OpenTelemetry, NIST, and the European Union recur across the lineage.

## What's actually new this time

The term “receipt” is not new, and neither are its proposed fields. Agent identity and responsibility map to W3C PROV; task traces map to Dapper and OpenTelemetry; portable signed action metadata maps to in-toto; immutable inclusion proofs map to Rekor and SCITT; short transparency documents map to ADRs, datasheets, and model cards. The 2026-04-13 SCITT agent-execution draft already combines many of these elements and even calls its output an Evidence Receipt. The exploration should therefore avoid claiming invention of the category. [W3C PROV](https://www.w3.org/TR/2013/NOTE-prov-overview-20130430/) [in-toto](https://github.com/in-toto/docs/blob/master/in-toto-spec.md) [SCITT agent-execution draft](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-emirdag-scitt-ai-agent-execution-00)

What is new is the execution shape. A conventional service follows code paths engineers deployed; an agent may choose tools and arguments at runtime, loop, retry, hand work to another agent, consume changing retrieved evidence, and take externally consequential actions. OpenAI’s tracing hierarchy now distinguishes task, agent, turn, generation, function, guardrail, and handoff spans, while A2A distinguishes task history, messages, and artifacts. That creates a new need to bind a human-scale task outcome to a machine-scale causal graph without pretending the summary is the graph. [OpenAI Agents SDK tracing](https://openai.github.io/openai-agents-js/guides/tracing/) [A2A specification](https://google-a2a.github.io/A2A/specification/)

The timing is also new. Runtime agent telemetry conventions remain in development at the same moment regulators require traceability and institutional standards programmes focus on agent identity and authorization. OpenTelemetry’s own agent-observability article described a draft framework convention; NIST’s initiative promises future guidelines rather than presenting a settled standard. This is a window in which small experiments can influence practice, but it is also a warning that field names chosen now may churn. [OpenTelemetry AI-agent observability](https://opentelemetry.io/blog/2025/ai-agent-observability/) [NIST AI Agent Standards Initiative](https://www.nist.gov/artificial-intelligence/ai-agent-standards-initiative)

The strongest genuinely useful framing is therefore not “logs versus receipt.” It is a three-layer record:

1. **Receipt:** a small, portable, human-readable manifest of task, authority, outcome, changes, verification, reversibility, cost, and evidence pointers.
2. **Trace:** detailed, queryable causal telemetry kept under proportionate retention and access controls.
3. **Attestation:** signatures, timestamps, append-only inclusion proofs, or independent custody establishing who asserted the record and whether it changed.

No single predecessor provides all three. Combining them while preserving selective disclosure, exit, and comprehensibility is the exploration’s real design space.

## What an experienced observer would expect next

First, the common layer will stay small while domain profiles grow around it. SLSA’s stable release became less ambitious after early feedback, and SCITT intentionally standardizes a thin signed-statement and receipt layer while leaving payload semantics to application domains. An experienced standards participant would expect a compact core—task ID, actor/operator identity, authorization reference, time, outcome, change/evidence digests, verifier, schema version, and trace pointer—with extensions for payments, code changes, healthcare, infrastructure, or communication. [SLSA stable-release rationale](https://slsa.dev/spec/v1.0/whats-new) [IETF SCITT architecture](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-ietf-scitt-architecture-22.html)

Second, capture will probably ride on OpenTelemetry or framework-native tracing, while trust is layered separately. OpenTelemetry already standardizes agent, conversation, model, tool, token, and evaluation attributes; OpenAI emits task-to-handoff span hierarchies. Neither claims to make those events independently true. A practical receipt prototype should correlate to a trace but let an independent tool or policy engine sign the high-consequence action and verification fields. [OpenTelemetry GenAI attributes](https://opentelemetry.io/docs/specs/semconv/registry/attributes/gen-ai/) [OpenAI Agents SDK tracing](https://openai.github.io/openai-agents-js/guides/tracing/)

Third, receipts will become risk-tiered rather than universal transcripts. The EU AI Act requires traceability appropriate to intended purpose, and its minimum retention rules apply to covered high-risk systems rather than every AI call. The SCITT agent draft similarly defines “Material Actions” and stronger identity levels. The likely pattern is sparse receipts for low-risk read-only work, signed change and approval evidence for reversible writes, and independently custodied, sequence-complete records for money movement, regulated decisions, or irreversible infrastructure changes. [EU AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=celex%3A32024R1689) [SCITT agent-execution draft](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-emirdag-scitt-ai-agent-execution-00)

Fourth, privacy will force references and commitments instead of copied content. Full prompts, retrieved documents, tool arguments, and outputs can contain personal data, secrets, privileged material, or employee-monitoring data. SCITT permits large or sensitive statements to be referenced by digest, and the agent-execution draft proposes redaction receipts and verification endpoints that do not reveal payloads. An experienced observer would expect evidence to remain in authoritative systems, with the receipt carrying durable identifiers, content hashes, access policy, and deletion state. [IETF SCITT architecture](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-ietf-scitt-architecture-22.html) [SCITT agent-execution privacy considerations](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-emirdag-scitt-ai-agent-execution-00)

Finally, the first generation will over-record. The history of security logging shows a repeated cycle: collect everything, discover cost and review limits, sample or normalize, then learn that the omitted event mattered. The better experiment is not to ask whether a YAML receipt looks complete. It is to give a reviewer a completed task, remove their platform access, and measure whether the receipt plus referenced evidence lets them correctly reconstruct authority, effects, verification, uncertainty, and reversal—and how long that takes compared with raw traces alone.
