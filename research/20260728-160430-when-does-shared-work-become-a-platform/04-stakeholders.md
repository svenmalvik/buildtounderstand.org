# Stakeholders, money, and power

The evidence does not support treating “the AI platform” as a neutral technical object. It is an internal allocation of work and authority wrapped around a commercial market in which the largest candidate suppliers earn tens of billions of dollars from cloud consumption ([Alphabet 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000018/goog-20251231.htm), [Amazon 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000004/amzn-20251231.htm), [Microsoft annual report](https://www.microsoft.com/investor/reports/ar25/index.html)). The cleanest empirical result is also the most inconvenient: a platform can improve perceived productivity while worsening delivery throughput and stability, especially when its use is mandatory ([DORA report](https://dora.dev/research/2024/dora-report/2024-dora-accelerate-state-of-devops-report.pdf)).

## Geography and jurisdiction

This section is Optional in the research plan, but it matters whenever a “small shared capability” sends prompts, code, telemetry, evaluation data, or credentials to an external AI platform.

| Layer | Location(s) | Jurisdiction(s) | Why it matters | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Subject HQ / corporate seat | n/a: there is no single subject company | The relevant corporate seat depends on the selected operator. | A build-versus-buy decision cannot inherit one jurisdictional answer before the vendor and contracting entity are selected. | [AWS contracting-party table](https://aws.amazon.com/agreement/) |
| Subject operating sites | Cloud regions and support locations selected or used by the operator | Region law plus the operator’s home and contracting-party law | A regional endpoint can constrain processing geography, but not every feature has identical residency controls. | [Google Cloud residency list](https://cloud.google.com/terms/data-residency), [Vertex AI security controls](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/security-controls) |
| Incorporation chain | Alphabet/Google, Amazon/AWS, Microsoft/Azure, or a private vendor chain | Primarily U.S. parent-company law plus local subsidiaries | The platform can be sold through a local entity while strategic control and capital allocation remain with a U.S. parent. The exact chain is vendor-specific, so no single chain can be asserted for this conceptual inquiry. | [Alphabet filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000018/goog-20251231.htm), [Amazon filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000004/amzn-20251231.htm) |
| Customer or counterparty location | Customer billing country and locations where outputs are used | Customer-country law; EU law can apply to output used in the Union | EU AI Act scope can reach third-country providers and deployers when system output is used in the EU. | [EU AI Act, Article 2](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32024R1689) |
| Governing law of contract | Depends on contracting party and account country | For AWS, examples include Luxembourg for AWS EMEA SARL and Washington for AWS, Inc.; Google applies regional modifications based on billing address | “Choose an EU region” does not itself determine the contract’s governing law. | [AWS agreement](https://aws.amazon.com/agreement/), [Google Cloud terms](https://cloud.google.com/terms) |
| Data residency at rest | Customer-selected supported region or multi-region | Local data-protection law plus contract commitments | Residency is feature-specific: Google lists GenAI services that support configured locations and exceptions such as some grounding and RAG features. | [Google Cloud residency list](https://cloud.google.com/terms/data-residency) |
| Data in transit, replicas, backups, and support access | Potentially multiple regions and support locations | Contract, data-protection law, and provider access policies | A statement about data “at rest” is narrower than a statement about all processing, caching, support access, or replicas. Google documents 24-hour in-memory caching by default for published Gemini models and feature-specific exceptions. | [Vertex AI zero-data-retention documentation](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/vertex-ai-zero-data-retention) |
| Compelled-access reach | Data in a provider’s possession, custody, or control, including data abroad for providers subject to U.S. jurisdiction | U.S. Stored Communications Act as amended by the CLOUD Act; executive-agreement partner law where applicable | Physical storage in Europe does not, by itself, eliminate lawful-access questions attached to provider control. | [CLOUD Act text](https://www.justice.gov/criminal/media/999391/dl), [U.S. Department of Justice guidance](https://www.justice.gov/criminal/criminal-oia/regarding-cloud-act-executive-agreements) |
| Sanctions, export controls, and dual use | Customer, user, chip, model, and destination locations | U.S. EAR and sanctions plus other applicable regimes | Access to advanced compute and some AI assets can be constrained by destination and end use; AWS also contractually places compliance for customer-selected use and regions on the customer. | [BIS 2025-01-15 rule announcement](https://www.bis.gov/press-release/commerce-strengthens-restrictions-advanced-computing-semiconductors-enhance-foundry-due-diligence-prevent), [AWS agreement](https://aws.amazon.com/agreement/) |
| Regulatory supervisors | EU AI Office, national market-surveillance and data-protection authorities; sector supervisors where applicable | EU and member-state law, plus sector-specific law | A shared platform may make controls easier to implement, but it also concentrates evidence and failures into a common control plane. The legal duties attach to the provider/deployer role, not to the internal label “platform.” | [EU AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32024R1689), [NIST Generative AI Profile](https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence) |

The central asymmetry is that storage location, contract law, operator control, and the place where outputs are used can all differ. An EU customer can select EU processing, contract with a Luxembourg entity, use a U.S.-controlled provider, and still bring an out-of-EU provider within EU AI Act scope when outputs are used in the Union; residency therefore answers only one part of the authority question ([AWS agreement](https://aws.amazon.com/agreement/), [EU AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32024R1689)).

The important accounting boundary is that token price is not platform cost. A defensible comparison must add internal platform labour, security and compliance work, data and evaluation pipelines, support and on-call, incident and rework cost, adoption effort, vendor-management time, and eventual migration or deletion; vendor pricing meters only enumerated services, while the DORA and PlatformEngineering.org evidence separately shows delivery effects, staffing, budget, feedback, and measurement burdens ([AWS pricing](https://aws.amazon.com/bedrock/pricing/), [DORA report](https://dora.dev/research/2024/dora-report/2024-dora-accelerate-state-of-devops-report.pdf), [PlatformEngineering.org](https://platformengineering.org/blog/announcing-the-state-of-platform-engineering-vol-4)). The available public sources do not provide one universal fully loaded figure, so none is invented here.

## Under-recognised winners and losers

Cloud vendors win quietly when an internal “platform” decision expands consumption across compute, storage, evaluation, observability, security, and support; Alphabet, Amazon, and Microsoft filings show why that expansion is financially material ([Alphabet 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000018/goog-20251231.htm), [Amazon 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000004/amzn-20251231.htm), [Microsoft annual report](https://www.microsoft.com/investor/reports/ar25/index.html)). Control functions can also win because platform defaults turn policy into executable guardrails, although they become exposed to common-mode control failures ([EU AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32024R1689)). Platform maintainers can lose without the loss appearing in a token-price comparison: they inherit product discovery, integration, on-call, upgrades, exception handling, documentation, and deprecation while nearly half of surveyed initiatives reported annual budgets no greater than $1 million ([PlatformEngineering.org](https://platformengineering.org/blog/announcing-the-state-of-platform-engineering-vol-4)). Engineers outside the golden path are exposed without meaningful consent when a leadership mandate removes the escape route; DORA’s 6% throughput association for exclusive use makes that a measurable governance issue, not merely a preference ([DORA PDF](https://dora.dev/research/2024/dora-report/2024-dora-accelerate-state-of-devops-report.pdf)). Finally, vendors and category publishers own much of the evidence used to justify the purchase, while the peer-reviewed base remains sparse ([Frontiers review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computer-science/articles/10.3389/fcomp.2026.1814498/full)).
