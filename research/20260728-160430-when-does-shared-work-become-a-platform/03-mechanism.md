# Mechanism: from repeated constraint to the smallest maintained capability

## What I'm unpacking

The mechanism is a decision-and-feedback loop that observes a repeated constraint in a valuable engineering workflow, selects the least powerful intervention that can remove it—documentation, convention, library, narrow service, or managed platform—then uses adoption, outcome, exception, and operating-cost evidence to delete, hold, or expand that intervention.

## The walkthrough

1. **Name one valuable workflow and its users.** The platform product owner maps a concrete journey such as “application engineer gets approved model access and ships an evaluated change,” including the start, successful end state, hand-offs, and people waiting in between. The input is interviews, support traffic, workflow telemetry, incidents, and business context; the output is a bounded workflow map with an accountable user group. DORA recommends mapping critical user journeys to find significant friction, while GitHub’s developer-experience work argues that system data should be paired with developer-reported experience because system metrics show *what* is slow but not necessarily *why* ([DORA](https://dora.dev/capabilities/platform-engineering/), [GitHub](https://resources.github.com/devsat-survey-measuring-developer-experience/)). **What can fail:** starting with a desired tool or portal and reverse-engineering a problem for it.

2. **Establish that the constraint repeats and matters.** The workflow owner records a baseline: affected teams and personas, frequency, waiting time, manual effort, error or incident rate, control duplication, model/token cost, task success, and perceived cognitive load. The output is not “engineers dislike this”; it is a dated evidence bundle that links a recurring obstruction to a valued outcome. Productivity cannot be represented by one activity measure, so the baseline should cover multiple dimensions rather than, for example, counting only deployments or portal visits ([Microsoft Research: SPACE](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-space-of-developer-productivity-theres-more-to-it-than-you-think/?msockid=0f7e7c38938d67a131406a7e92a666cf), [Google Research: HEART](https://research.google.com/pubs/archive/36299.pdf)). **What can fail:** treating repetition, annoyance, or executive urgency as proof of material constraint.

3. **Diagnose the constraint before selecting its container.** The platform and application teams determine whether the obstruction is primarily missing knowledge, inconsistent local choices, duplicated deterministic code, a runtime coordination or enforcement need, or an undifferentiated commodity capability. The output is a diagnosis and at least one credible non-platform alternative. Team Topologies explicitly allows a thinnest viable platform to be only a wiki page, while Microsoft’s current AI Gateway guidance says a single application calling one model without shared governance or credentials may be simpler when it calls the provider directly ([Team Topologies](https://teamtopologies.com/key-concepts-content/what-is-a-thinnest-viable-platform-tvp), [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/api-management/ai-gateway-overview)). **What can fail:** assuming that “shared” means “runtime service,” or that every AI workload needs an AI gateway.

4. **Write a falsifiable intervention hypothesis.** The owner states: “For persona P performing workflow W, intervention I should move baseline measures B by an agreed amount within a defined pilot period, without worsening guardrail measures G.” Inputs are the workflow map, baseline, risk limits, and available operating capacity; output is a hypothesis with a rollback condition. This matters because the available platform-engineering evidence is still unusually gray-literature-heavy: a 2026-05-04 multivocal review found only 2 of 88 included sources were tier-one publications primarily about platform engineering, and it found no peer-reviewed effectiveness evidence for portal scorecards despite their commercial use ([Frontiers in Computer Science](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computer-science/articles/10.3389/fcomp.2026.1814498/full)). **What can fail:** declaring success criteria after rollout, when adoption and sunk cost can influence the definition.

5. **Choose the least powerful intervention that satisfies the hypothesis.** The team uses the escalation ladder below and stops as soon as an option can plausibly remove the measured constraint. Documentation or a convention communicates; a library distributes versioned reusable logic; a narrow service centralises state, secrets, policy, quotas, routing, or telemetry; a managed platform transfers more operation to a provider; an internal platform composes multiple capabilities into a maintained self-service journey. AWS’s own decision guide distinguishes managed foundation-model inference in Bedrock from the broader model building, training, and deployment scope in SageMaker AI, demonstrating that even a vendor’s portfolio has workload-specific boundaries rather than one universal platform ([AWS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/decision-guides/latest/bedrock-or-sagemaker/bedrock-or-sagemaker.html)). **What can fail:** selecting the broadest future-proof architecture and thereby paying for hypothetical use cases.

6. **Build a reversible pilot with real users.** One or two willing teams use the intervention on real, noncritical work; the platform team supplies documentation, migration help, a support channel, observability, an owner, and an explicit route back to the previous path. The output is a working slice plus pilot evidence, not a company-wide launch. CNCF’s maturity model treats natural pull and user feedback as stronger signals than mandated adoption, and Microsoft advises controlled pilots and rollback plans for its preview AI Gateway tier because it has no service-level agreement and its APIs, limits, pricing, and regions can change ([CNCF TAG App Delivery](https://tag-app-delivery.cncf.io/fr/whitepapers/platform-eng-maturity-model/), [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/api-management/ai-gateway-overview)). **What can fail:** forcing a mandate before fit is known, or piloting only with the platform team’s most sympathetic users.

7. **Operate the capability as a product and as production software.** The named owner maintains the interface, support path, security model, service levels, cost attribution, upgrade path, and provider dependencies. The output is a supportable service or artifact with Day-2 responsibilities made visible. Spotify’s Backstage adoption guidance says a central team must maintain the deployment, infrastructure and CI/CD, provide customer support, drive adoption, and eventually provide on-call support; these are costs of the platform, not implementation footnotes ([Backstage](https://backstage.io/docs/overview/adopting/)). **What can fail:** a “self-service” facade whose failures still require tickets or application teams to understand every hidden layer.

8. **Measure task outcome, adoption quality, and system health together.** Instrument the agreed workflow before and after use: task time and success, wait states, change stability and throughput, reliability, cost per successful outcome, support burden, adoption, retention, bypass rate, and user-reported load. For AI capabilities, add a versioned evaluation dataset and inspect production failures; OpenAI’s evaluation flywheel uses qualitative failure analysis, a measured baseline, targeted improvement, and repeated evaluation rather than one launch-time benchmark ([OpenAI Cookbook](https://github.com/openai/openai-cookbook/blob/main/examples/evaluation/Building_resilient_prompts_using_an_evaluation_flywheel.md)). DORA reports that internal platforms can improve productivity and organisational performance while decreasing change stability and throughput, which is why a single positive metric is insufficient ([DORA](https://dora.dev/research/2024/dora-report/)). **What can fail:** counting logins, calls, templates created, or AI-generated output as value while downstream delivery worsens.

9. **Treat exceptions as diagnostic data.** The platform publishes the golden path, the automated guardrails, recovery safety nets, and the cases requiring manual judgment; every bypass records persona, workload, reason, delay, and eventual resolution. The output is an exception register that distinguishes legitimate workload diversity from missing platform capability, poor documentation, or unsafe requests. Google’s control taxonomy separates steering through golden paths, hard guardrails, recovery-oriented safety nets, and manual checkpoints; it also warns that different controls serve different purposes ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/application-modernization/platform-engineering-control-mechanisms)). **What can fail:** turning every exception into a platform feature, or making escape so painful that teams hide deviations.

10. **Make an explicit delete, hold, buy, or expand decision.** At a scheduled review, compare avoided waiting, duplicated work, errors and risk with build/run cost, support load, adoption cost, exception load, provider cost, and switching exposure. Delete or demote the capability when the original constraint is gone, a commodity alternative is better, voluntary use and task success remain weak, or required reinvestment exceeds demonstrated value; hold it when one workflow is served; expand only when an adjacent repeated constraint is independently evidenced. The Government Digital Service followed this logic when it announced on 2022-07-12 that GOV.UK PaaS had not achieved continued growth and faced major architectural investment, so it chose sunset while explicitly considering alternatives ranging from doing nothing to reusable components or a new PaaS ([Government Digital Service](https://gds.blog.gov.uk/2022/07/12/why-weve-decided-to-decommission-gov-uk-paas-platform-as-a-service/)). **What can fail:** allowing sunk cost, organisational status, or a vendor contract to turn the platform into a permanent default.

## Internals where most coverage waves hands

### 1. The constraint has to be qualified, not merely repeated

The actionable unit is a **repeated, consequential wait or risk in a named workflow**, not duplicated code by itself. Repetition becomes platform evidence when the same obstruction appears across users, its cost can be observed, and a central intervention can plausibly remove it without creating a larger dependency. This is a proposed operational test, not an empirically validated universal threshold: the 2026-05-04 multivocal review found platform engineering’s formal evidence base sparse and identified the lack of validated measurement instruments as a major gap ([Frontiers in Computer Science](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computer-science/articles/10.3389/fcomp.2026.1814498/full)).

The evidence bundle should therefore contain both sides of the workflow. Upstream measures include request frequency, hand-off wait, repeated implementation effort, policy-review delay, failed access requests, or model-cost variance. Downstream measures include successful delivery, change stability, recovery, customer outcome, and user-reported cognitive load. DORA’s finding that platforms can improve perceived productivity while harming throughput or stability is a concrete reason to keep both sides visible ([DORA](https://dora.dev/research/2024/dora-report/)).

### 2. The intervention ladder is about coordination power

The following is a decision test synthesised from thinnest-viable-platform guidance, managed-service decision guides, and current AI-gateway documentation; it is not a published industry standard.

| Observed constraint | Smallest candidate | What it coordinates | Escalate only when |
|---|---|---|---|
| People do not know the approved choice or sequence, and drift is tolerable | Documentation or decision record | Shared knowledge | The same ambiguity persists despite findable, current documentation and feedback. Team Topologies says a wiki page can be the whole thinnest viable platform ([Team Topologies](https://teamtopologies.com/key-concepts-content/what-is-a-thinnest-viable-platform-tvp)). |
| Teams make avoidably different choices, but runtime enforcement is unnecessary | Convention, template, or CI check | Shared default | Teams repeatedly misapply the default, or the risk requires automated prevention rather than guidance. Google distinguishes steering golden paths from hard guardrails and human checkpoints ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/application-modernization/platform-engineering-control-mechanisms)). |
| The same deterministic transformation or client behaviour is copied into codebases | Versioned library or SDK | Reusable implementation | Central state, secret custody, cross-team quota, routing, or uniform runtime policy is required. |
| Multiple applications need shared credentials, quotas, routing, policy or telemetry | Narrow service such as an AI gateway | Runtime state and enforcement | Several capabilities must form one coherent self-service journey and the organisation can sustain a product team. Microsoft says direct provider access can be simpler for one app and one model, while a gateway becomes relevant for many consumers, models, credentials, and policies ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/api-management/ai-gateway-overview)). |
| The required capability is commodity and a provider meets control, reliability, cost, residency and exit needs | Managed service | Operation transferred by contract | Differentiating logic or an unmet control cannot be supplied by the managed option. AWS separates managed inference needs from broader model-building and hosting needs rather than prescribing one service ([AWS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/decision-guides/latest/bedrock-or-sagemaker/bedrock-or-sagemaker.html)). |
| Several proven capabilities serve related journeys, and users repeatedly ask for consistent discovery and self-service | Internal platform | A portfolio of product capabilities | Expansion produces measured net improvement and does not turn the central team into ticket operations. CNCF treats intrinsic user pull, not mandate, as the stronger maturity signal ([CNCF TAG App Delivery](https://tag-app-delivery.cncf.io/fr/whitepapers/platform-eng-maturity-model/)). |

The important internal rule is **stop escalation when the outcome moves**. A working library does not need to become a service because centralisation looks tidier; a working narrow gateway does not need a portal because portals are fashionable.

### 3. “Adoption” is a composite signal, not a login count

Adoption has at least four layers: awareness, first successful task, retained use for the target workflow, and voluntary preference over an available alternative. HEART separates adoption, retention, and task success, while CNCF’s maturity model treats self-sustaining pull as distinct from use caused by mandate ([Google Research](https://research.google.com/pubs/archive/36299.pdf), [CNCF TAG App Delivery](https://tag-app-delivery.cncf.io/fr/whitepapers/platform-eng-maturity-model/)). A practical cohort view should therefore track: eligible users, attempted users, successful users, repeat users, bypassing users, and churned users—by persona and workload.

Spotify’s own Backstage guidance illustrates both the value and danger of proxies: it publishes onboarding, contribution, and usage measures, but explicitly warns that time spent in Backstage is time not spent writing code ([Backstage](https://backstage.io/docs/overview/adopting/)). The platform owner should connect adoption to a completed workflow, not optimise screen time or API-call volume.

### 4. Measurement needs a counterfactual and a guardrail

A before/after line is weak when workload mix, staffing, models, organisational priorities, or user skill change at the same time. The feasible internal design is usually a staged rollout with matched teams or workflows, a fixed observation window, predeclared outcome and guardrail measures, and qualitative review of failures. It will not eliminate selection bias, but it makes the assumptions inspectable. The SPACE framework rejects single-metric productivity, and Google’s HEART paper says product metrics should be triangulated with other evidence rather than standing alone ([Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-space-of-developer-productivity-theres-more-to-it-than-you-think/?msockid=0f7e7c38938d67a131406a7e92a666cf), [Google Research](https://research.google.com/pubs/archive/36299.pdf)).

AI makes the counterfactual especially unstable. METR’s 2025-07-10 randomised study found experienced open-source developers slower with early-2025 tools even though they perceived speedup, but METR’s 2026-02-24 follow-up said newer estimates were unreliable because of selection effects, changed compensation, task selection, and difficulty measuring parallel agent work ([METR study](https://metr.org/research/), [METR update](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/)). The correct lesson is not a timeless speedup or slowdown number; it is that felt productivity, task time, user selection, tool generation, and downstream outcomes can diverge.

### 5. Exceptions are where platform scope is discovered

An exception request should carry the target workflow, rejected platform path, missing capability or conflicting constraint, risk classification, owner, decision, time-to-decision, and whether the resolution becomes a one-off, documentation change, library change, platform change, or evidence that the workload should stay outside. Google’s separation of golden paths, guardrails, safety nets, and manual checkpoints provides the control vocabulary: a preference should not be enforced like a catastrophic-risk stop, and an inherently judgmental decision should not be hidden behind brittle automation ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/application-modernization/platform-engineering-control-mechanisms)).

Two ratios expose opposite pathologies: **bypass rate** can show poor fit, while **platform-change-per-exception** can show indiscriminate expansion. Neither ratio has a validated universal target in the reviewed research; the recent literature review explicitly identifies the absence of platform-specific validated measurement instruments ([Frontiers in Computer Science](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computer-science/articles/10.3389/fcomp.2026.1814498/full)).

### 6. Deletion is part of the architecture

Every capability needs an owner, review date, dependency map, data export, compatibility contract, rollback path, and deletion trigger when it is introduced. This turns deletion from an emergency migration into a normal branch of the feedback loop. GOV.UK PaaS demonstrates that a useful, reliable platform can still reach a rational sunset when growth stalls, users acquire alternative capability, providers improve, and the next architectural investment is too large; GDS reported 60-plus public bodies, 172 digital services, 3,200 applications, 99.95% uptime, and only one major incident, yet still chose decommissioning on 2022-07-12 ([Government Digital Service](https://gds.blog.gov.uk/2022/07/12/why-weve-decided-to-decommission-gov-uk-paas-platform-as-a-service/)).

Expansion should have the same burden of proof as creation. CNCF advises using platform-usage data to allocate funds and staff and treats new capability demand as meaningful when it follows positive experience with existing capabilities ([CNCF TAG App Delivery](https://tag-app-delivery.cncf.io/fr/whitepapers/platform-eng-maturity-model/)). The proposed governance rule is therefore: an adjacent feature request enters the loop as a new constraint hypothesis; it does not inherit justification from the platform’s existence.

## Failure modes

| Failure mode | How it breaks the loop | Historical example |
|---|---|---|
| **A solution is built for a constraint that no longer dominates.** | Adoption remains weak or the market and internal capability overtake the original design, but the organisation keeps funding it because it already exists. | On 2022-07-12, GDS said cloud providers had lowered barriers, departments had developed cloud capability, GOV.UK PaaS lacked continuing growth, and the alternative was heavy architectural investment; it chose sunset ([GDS](https://gds.blog.gov.uk/2022/07/12/why-weve-decided-to-decommission-gov-uk-paas-platform-as-a-service/)). |
| **The platform creates a shared blast radius.** | A common control-plane or telemetry change can degrade every dependent workflow at once. | On 2024-12-11, a new OpenAI telemetry service overwhelmed the Kubernetes control plane and caused cascading degradation or unavailability across OpenAI services ([OpenAI Status](https://status.openai.com/incidents/ctrsv3lwd797)). |
| **A narrow dependency silently becomes critical infrastructure.** | Many capabilities depend on one store, gateway, identity layer, or provider; local resilience assumptions do not survive a dependency outage. | On 2025-06-12, failure in storage backing Cloudflare Workers KV affected Workers AI, Access, Gateway, AutoRAG and other services globally for 2 hours 28 minutes; Cloudflare said it remained responsible for the chosen dependency architecture ([Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-service-outage-june-12-2025/)). |
| **Supplier terms change after the platform is embedded.** | A licence, price, region, quota, or API change makes the current substrate unacceptable and turns portability into urgent migration work. | HashiCorp changed Terraform from MPLv2 to Business Source License 1.1 on 2023-08-10; the ecosystem response produced the OpenTofu fork under Linux Foundation governance ([HashiCorp](https://www.hashicorp.com/en/blog/hashicorp-adopts-business-source-license), [Linux Foundation](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/announcing-opentofu)). HashiCorp states that internal use remains permitted, so the direct impact varies by user; the case still demonstrates dependency-policy volatility. |
| **A proxy says “better” while the workflow says “worse.”** | Perceived productivity, portal usage, lines of code, or generated output rises while task completion, quality, or downstream delivery does not. | METR’s 2025-07-10 study found participants perceived an AI speedup while measured completion time increased; on 2026-02-24 METR also disclosed that its newer estimates were unreliable because selection and measurement had changed ([METR research](https://metr.org/research/), [METR update](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/)). |
| **Productivity is improved locally but throughput or stability degrades systemically.** | The platform optimises developer convenience or one team’s task while adding change coupling, batch size, or unstable controls downstream. | DORA’s platform-engineering analysis reports positive associations with individual, team and organisational performance alongside cautionary decreases in change stability and throughput ([DORA](https://dora.dev/research/2024/dora-report/)). The report page is a cross-sectional research result, not a named-company postmortem, so it supports the risk pattern rather than a causal incident claim. |
| **The “self-service tool” becomes a second product estate.** | Maintenance, upgrades, support and on-call load consume the platform team, while application teams still need help using it. | Spotify’s Backstage adoption documentation—date not stated—requires a central owner for deployment maintenance, infrastructure, CI/CD, support, adoption and eventual on-call; Spotify’s separate managed Portal product explicitly sells reduced setup and ongoing maintenance burden ([Backstage](https://backstage.io/docs/overview/adopting/), [Spotify Portal](https://backstage.spotify.com/docs/portal)). This is vendor-authored operating evidence, not an independent cost comparison. |
| **Governance automation is mistaken for evidence of governance outcomes.** | Passing a scorecard or policy check becomes the goal even when it has not been shown to reduce incidents, risk, or review time. | The platform-engineering review published on 2026-05-04 found no peer-reviewed empirical evidence for IDP scorecard effectiveness despite broad commercial adoption ([Frontiers in Computer Science](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computer-science/articles/10.3389/fcomp.2026.1814498/full)). This is an evidence-gap example, not proof that scorecards fail. |

## Constraints and ceilings

- **There is no evidenced universal “N teams” threshold.** The strongest defensible boundary found is contextual: Microsoft says direct provider access can be simpler for one application and one model without shared governance, while Team Topologies allows the thinnest platform to be documentation; the recent academic review says validated platform-specific measurement remains missing ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/api-management/ai-gateway-overview), [Team Topologies](https://teamtopologies.com/key-concepts-content/what-is-a-thinnest-viable-platform-tvp), [Frontiers in Computer Science](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computer-science/articles/10.3389/fcomp.2026.1814498/full)).
- **Maintainer capacity is a hard organisational ceiling.** A capability is not viable if no team can support, secure, upgrade, observe, document and retire it. Backstage’s own operating model includes infrastructure, CI/CD, support, adoption, metrics, and on-call work ([Backstage](https://backstage.io/docs/overview/adopting/)).
- **Workload diversity limits abstraction.** Google says there is no one-size-fits-all platform and that golden paths must be co-designed with developers; an interface that hides legitimate workload differences becomes a golden cage or grows escape hatches ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/solutions/platform-engineering)).
- **AI model and provider volatility limit stable abstractions.** Microsoft’s AI Gateway tier is in public preview without an SLA and with changeable APIs, pricing, limits and regions; an internal contract may damp provider change, but the platform team must absorb and test that change ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/api-management/ai-gateway-overview)).
- **Quotas and latency remain physical service limits.** A gateway can allocate tokens, cache, load-balance, and open circuit breakers, but it cannot create backend model capacity; Microsoft explicitly says gateway scaling must be paired with backend scaling and distribution ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-ie/azure/api-management/genai-gateway-capabilities)).
- **Reliability is bounded by the dependency graph.** Cloudflare’s 2025-06-12 incident shows that a third-party-backed shared store can propagate failure into many higher-level services even when the triggering outage is outside the platform operator ([Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-service-outage-june-12-2025/)).
- **Data handling and jurisdiction constrain central telemetry.** Microsoft says AI data residency depends on the complete request path across gateway, backend and telemetry destinations; OpenTelemetry notes that captured prompts, completions, tool arguments and results can contain sensitive data and therefore are off by default in its example ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/api-management/ai-gateway-overview), [OpenTelemetry](https://opentelemetry.io/blog/2026/genai-observability/)).
- **Economics change over time.** GOV.UK PaaS remained reliable and widely used but faced a new investment boundary after providers and departmental skills improved; a platform’s original build decision does not settle its later maintain-versus-delete decision ([Government Digital Service](https://gds.blog.gov.uk/2022/07/12/why-weve-decided-to-decommission-gov-uk-paas-platform-as-a-service/)).

## Side effects

| Side effect | Mechanism and significance |
|---|---|
| **Coordination moves rather than disappears.** | Application teams do less provider, policy, and infrastructure integration; the platform team inherits interface design, support, upgrades, reliability and deprecation. This is an inference from the operating duties in Backstage’s own adoption guidance ([Backstage](https://backstage.io/docs/overview/adopting/)). |
| **A new source of organisational telemetry appears.** | A gateway can attribute token use, latency, errors and quotas by consumer, which enables cost allocation and debugging but also makes central surveillance and metric gaming possible ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-ie/azure/api-management/genai-gateway-capabilities)). |
| **Sensitive data may be newly concentrated.** | Central prompt, completion and tool traces make evaluation and debugging easier while increasing the value and risk of the telemetry store; OpenTelemetry leaves full-content capture disabled by default in its worked example ([OpenTelemetry](https://opentelemetry.io/blog/2026/genai-observability/)). |
| **Standardisation creates both leverage and coupling.** | Common interfaces and conventions reduce duplicated integration but create a shared change schedule and common blast radius, as the OpenAI telemetry-control-plane and Cloudflare Workers KV incidents illustrate ([OpenAI Status](https://status.openai.com/incidents/ctrsv3lwd797), [Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-service-outage-june-12-2025/)). |
| **Control functions gain enforceable leverage.** | Security, finance and compliance can move from advisory review to runtime credentials, quotas, filters, logging and hard stops at a gateway ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-ie/azure/api-management/genai-gateway-capabilities)). This can shorten repeated review, but an incorrectly scoped policy can block every tenant. |
| **Local agency can rise or fall.** | Self-service can remove waits, while mandate and insufficient escape paths can replace local technical choice with central approval. CNCF treats voluntary pull as a maturity signal, and Google distinguishes a guiding golden path from a guardrail that stops action ([CNCF TAG App Delivery](https://tag-app-delivery.cncf.io/fr/whitepapers/platform-eng-maturity-model/), [Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/application-modernization/platform-engineering-control-mechanisms)). |
| **An exit option acquires option value.** | Portable interfaces, data exports and rollback paths may seem unused until a provider changes licence or terms; the Terraform-to-OpenTofu response is a concrete instance of ecosystem investment in that option ([HashiCorp](https://www.hashicorp.com/en/blog/hashicorp-adopts-business-source-license), [Linux Foundation](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/announcing-opentofu)). |

## Plain-language analogy

Treat the organisation like a town deciding how to cross a stream. A sign is documentation; painted stepping points are conventions; a kit each household can reuse is a library; a maintained footbridge is a narrow shared service; and a staffed transport network is a platform. Count who is actually blocked, how often, and what the delay costs before building. Start with the least structure that lets people cross safely, watch whether they choose it and still reach their destination faster, record why some cannot use it, and remove or enlarge it only from that evidence. The analogy breaks because software dependencies, model behaviour, security policy, data residency and vendor terms can change underneath the crossing, while a bridge’s physical contract is comparatively stable.
